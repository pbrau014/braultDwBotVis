# v3.20 Three-Perspective Editorial Review

**Status:** Editorial successor candidate; not approved for event publication or live use. This review distinguishes documented facts from interpretations of tone, structure, and likely reader reaction. It is a critique of operational documentation, not a claim that the material is conventional narrative fiction.

## 1. Literary critic and structural analyst

### Underlying message and subtext
The strongest implied message is that fairness depends on evidence, explicit authority, and repeatable procedure rather than trust in a person, bot, calculator, or informal consensus. The repeated distinction among accepted performance metrics, participant-level scores, the calculated match outcome, bracket advancement, evidence capture, and implementation status communicates a healthy distrust of single-source certainty while making the calculation-to-outcome relationship explicit.

The accidental counter-theme is institutional fragility: the documents repeatedly warn that the system can fail when ownership, authoritative inputs, event settings, or technical artifacts are missing. The volume of decision registers and reconciliation records can make the governance apparatus feel more mature than the underlying implementation evidence. That is not proof of bad intent; it is a risk in how the package may be perceived.

### Bias and emotional undertone
The voice favors auditability, risk control, and procedural defensibility. That is appropriate for event operations, but it can sound defensive and bureaucratic to participants. A skeptical reader may infer that the organizers anticipate disputes or do not fully trust their own tooling. The most effective remedy is not softer language; it is concise explanations of what the rule protects, who decides, and what a participant should do next.

### Structural weaknesses
- Repeated warnings about source-of-truth boundaries are necessary, but repetition across guides, registers, and QA records risks diluting the highest-priority warnings.
- The technical reference contains historical examples, policy decisions, unresolved implementation work, and validation records. Those are different kinds of information and can blur together unless labels and signposting remain prominent.
- The participant-facing guide mixes practical play advice with governance and evidence policy. The distinction is useful but needs clear labels so strategy advice is not mistaken for tournament law.
- M06 is appropriately treated as a hypothesis rather than a proven finding. The package should not let the confident wording of the claim outrun the evidence supporting it.

### Editorial conclusion
The underlying purpose is coherent: make decisions reproducible and disputes resolvable. The chief literary weakness is not lack of a story arc; it is that process can overshadow the human task it serves. Keep additions short, local, and action-oriented.

## 2. Acquisitions editor: candid assessment

**Acquisition verdict:** I would not reject this material because it lacks a clear purpose. I would defer acceptance as a finished, production-ready package because it is dense, internally cross-referential, and still dependent on unavailable canonical technical artifacts and human approvals.

### What works
- The distinction among participant metrics, calculated individual outputs, the verified performance-based match outcome, bracket advancement, and separate awards is a strong governing structure—provided the approved equation clearly shows how the metrics determine the outcome.
- Explicit `BLOCKED`, `NOT TESTED`, `NO-GO`, and `NOT APPROVED` statuses protect credibility.
- Decision registers, checklists, evidence labels, and scenario examples support operational use.
- The package acknowledges the difference between a documented policy and an implemented, tested behavior.

### Where it loses grip
- **Pacing:** repeated caveats can slow the reader before the immediate action is clear. Lead with the action, then give the exception and evidence boundary.
- **Density:** many IDs and appendices are useful for maintainers but impose a high navigation cost on participants. Cross-reference only when it changes the next action.
- **Audience shifts:** participant instructions, operator procedures, engineering validation, and release governance sometimes sit close together. Label the audience and status before the details.
- **Closure:** a complete editorial package can feel like a completed system even when the workbook, HTML calculator, runtime, and release approvals are not verified. The release posture must remain impossible to misread.

### Primary reason for rejection
If submitted as a final production release, the primary rejection reason would be **evidence and release readiness, not editorial quality**: the canonical workbook and Score Reporter HTML are unavailable, some technical behavior is untested, and event-specific approvals remain open. If submitted as an editorial successor candidate with those blockers clearly declared, the material is substantially more defensible.

### Minimal revision policy
Do not rewrite for style alone. Keep changes where they resolve a real ambiguity, clarify authority, prevent a false inference, or make the next action easier to identify. The new quick hints follow that rule.

## 3. Skeptical first-time reader

### Immediate gut reaction
The package appears serious and unusually careful about fairness, but a newcomer may wonder why so many safeguards are needed and which document they must obey first. Repeated references to unavailable sources and untested tools may undermine confidence unless the package plainly explains what is safe to do now and what must wait.

### Likely confusion points
1. **Which rule governs this event?** The distinction between pilot and recurring formats is critical. The event notice and approved settings should be the first decision point, not an appendix discovered later.
2. **What decides who advances?** The verified match outcome governs bracket advancement, and the outcome is calculated from individual performance across the applicable published metrics (including Damage and Kills) under the approved scoring method and tie-break rules. The role of `dblns` must be stated through the approved formula, not treated as categorically unrelated to the outcome.
3. **Can I trust a displayed score?** A display, archived result, or static inspection is not equivalent to a verified calculation against the canonical workbook. The reader needs a clear status and escalation path.
4. **What is historical versus current?** Older test results and baseline documents can look authoritative. Their historical role must be explicitly stated wherever a reader might mistake them for current proof.
5. **Who can approve release?** A completed checklist or publication form is not itself approval. Missing signatures, event settings, and technical evidence must remain visible blockers.

### Narrative flow and failure points
The practical flow should be: identify the event format → check authority and eligibility → prepare the room → capture evidence → verify the official result → resolve disputes → publish only after the release gate. The package contains these elements, but its reference-heavy structure can make the reader reconstruct that sequence. The section-level hints reinforce the next action without replacing the underlying text.

### Reader verdict
I can understand the governing intent, but I would not infer that the technical system is ready merely because the documentation is extensive. I would want a one-page event-specific entry point, explicit status labels, and a named escalation path before participating or operating the event.

## 4. Reconciliation: reader comprehension governs

When the perspectives conflict, prioritize comprehension and operational accuracy. Preserve useful complexity where it records real authority, evidence, or safety boundaries. Avoid deleting necessary nuance merely to make the text shorter. Do not convert interpretive criticism into factual assertions about author intent.

Applied changes in this successor:
- Added compact ⚓ “Quick hint” callouts at main section boundaries in eligible current Markdown and Word documents.
- Added a few concise, context-specific clarifications to the three principal publications, without removing source text.
- Preserved the historical baselines and existing version identities as source material; this successor has its own v3.20 editorial-candidate identity.
- Did not create or use any new images; callouts use a text icon.

## 5. Technical and production boundary

This is an editorial production candidate, not authorization for live use. The canonical Excel workbook and authoritative Score Reporter HTML remain unavailable in the source package. Therefore workbook calculation parity, HTML interaction behavior, Excel/VBA/Office 2010 behavior, and other source-dependent tests remain **BLOCKED / NOT TESTED**. No historical test is represented as a new v3.20 test. Event-specific settings, approvals, and required release evidence must still be completed by the responsible humans.

## 6. Limitations of this review

The critique evaluates the package’s documented content, structure, and likely reader experience. “Implied message,” “defensive tone,” and “reader confidence” are editorial interpretations, not verified claims about the author’s private intent or every reader’s reaction. The quick hints are guidance aids and do not supersede governing rules, source documents, or approved event settings.
