The detailed recurring rules are the normal source for regular-season and postseason events. Pilot exceptions do not automatically apply. A published, authorized event-specific variation applies only to the named event/stage and must identify the changed rule and effective time.

### Source-derived recurring baseline — not an issued schedule

The source describes a weekly event with three normal rounds, roughly seven games per participant, 10-minute standard matches, 10 minutes between games, and a 15-minute break between rounds. The exact schedule table contains blank dates/times, so these are planning figures, not booked times.

- **Registration and teams:** signup is by person each week; the source uses an organizer-managed random team draft and says participants may not choose teammates. It lists a 30-day Starfleet-clan-tenure condition with exceptions, but that condition is **not an approved current eligibility rule** until the organizer explicitly confirms it in the dated settings. Publish registration window and roster lock; source placeholders remain incomplete.
- **Match composition:** source baseline says the first round is two human players against six bots. Later rounds may use 6v6 or 9v9 sides with two or three humans per side and bots filling the roster; it also says sides must not field fewer than six total players. The exact number of human/bot slots by stage must be explicitly published; do not infer it from abbreviated source wording.
- **Ships:** source lists destroyers and cruisers with torpedoes up to Tier IV, battleships and cruisers without torpedoes up to Tier V; player carriers and submarines are prohibited, though bots may include carriers. It also bans test, Early Access, purchased/unobtainable ships and allows event-specific ship bans. The source's ban/playable lists are blank, so a current approved allowable/banned-ship list is still required.
- **Maps and Race event — selected template policy:** the recurring map table lists Islands (weeks 1 and 8), Polar (2 and 9), Big Race (3 and 10), Archipelago (4 and 11), Ring (5), Straight (6), New Dawn (7), and Ocean (week 12), mostly Standard Battle for 10 minutes. “Big Race” is treated as a map name. Race is a separate 30-minute Ocean side-event, one hour before the regular event in weeks 3, 6, 9 and the finale (week 12 only if a 12-week season is approved). Race damage enters Race/cumulative season totals and a declared Race award, not that evening’s regular match score. The real calendar and this template policy require organizer ratification. The special armor-mode label conflicts in the source (“Armor Resilience” vs “Armor Resilient Test”); do not announce that special mode until the installed client confirms the exact label. If it cannot be verified, the organizer must approve omitting that game and the event should use only verified Standard Battle settings in the interim.

## 2. Registration, eligibility, identity and rosters

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Confirm the declared event format before applying eligibility, roster, timing, or award rules.</div>
1. Publish eligibility and account/platform requirements before registration opens. Do not import historical account age, battle-count, win-rate, clan-tenure, or press-account thresholds as general requirements.
2. Use individual signup by default. Collect in-game name, Discord name or declared alias, availability, and required eligibility confirmations. Publish opening/closing times, waitlist, duplicate registration, correction and final-roster publication rules.
3. The organizer makes an auditable team draft/assignment by default. A fixed-team format is permitted only if announced before signup. Publish team size, substitutes, bot usage if any, and minimum legal lineup.
4. Lock rosters before the first game. After lock, changes require an authorized ruling with reason and an updated official record. A substitute cannot be used to evade eligibility or a match result.
5. Publish the in-game/Discord identity mapping. Correct a mismatch before the player participates.

## 3. Pairings, series and advancement

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Use only the authorized tool and permissions; retain a manual fallback and never expose credentials.</div>
1. Publish stages, series length, pairing/seeding method, bye/withdrawal rules, consolation placement, and finale rules before registration. If series length is not published, the default is one game rather than an invented best-of format.
2. Recurring bracket advancement follows the verified match outcome, normally calculated from individual performance across the published metrics (including Damage and Kills) under the approved scoring method. The event must define the accepted inputs, formula, aggregation, rounding, and tie-breaks before play. A published forfeit/void rule may set the outcome when ordinary performance scoring does not apply. No single raw metric or overview average may silently replace the approved calculation.
3. Resolve tied standings in this order: (1) head-to-head result; (2) record among tied teams; (3) a declared team metric/point differential; (4) an extra deciding game if feasible; (5) an auditable random draw only when expressly authorized in the published rules.
4. Individual performance scoring contributes to the match outcome as defined by the approved published formula. It may also affect an award or seeding only when event settings define that additional use in advance. Once verified and recorded, a completed match outcome is changed only through the documented dispute/correction procedure, not by an unlogged recalculation.
5. If a match outcome is disputed, required evidence is missing, or participant metrics/calculator outputs disagree, pause outcome publication and bracket advancement and escalate to the referee. Reconcile inputs, formula/version, aggregation, and rounding before certifying the outcome; no previous winner is silently retained or reversed without an authorized, recorded correction.

## 4. Training Room, host and ready-up

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Verify room configuration and both sides’ readiness before starting; record exceptions as they occur.</div>
1. The published fixture identifies the game ID, home/away sides, host and fallback host, map/mode, battle duration, ship/tier limits, lineup visibility, observers, and settings version.
2. The Home Team hosts by default; in a published bracket, this is normally the team in the top slot unless the fixture states otherwise. The fallback host takes over if the assigned host cannot create the room promptly.
3. The host configures the room and places the lineups according to the selected format. In the normal detailed format, keep lineups hidden until both sides and slots are organized.
4. Both team representatives verify map, mode, duration, sides/spawns, ship limits, lineup legality/visibility, observer permissions and the assigned match. Resolve objections before ready-up.
5. Use a fixed map rotation unless a published pick/ban or random-selection process defines order, deadlines and fallback. Do not mix map-selection methods.

## 5. Timing, absence, restart and disputes

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Apply the declared clock and restart rule for this event; record the trigger, time, and decision.</div>
1. Publish separate clocks for the scheduled start/no-show wait, between-game wait, restart request cutoff, and restart completion. The recurring source baseline uses a 10-minute attendance wait; confirm the event’s chosen values.
2. If neither side is ready by the published deadline, record the published double-forfeit outcome. If one side is ready and the other is absent/not ready, apply the published default. Verified platform faults may justify a documented extension.
3. Publish the restart quota by series length. The source baseline allows one ordinary restart per team in Bo1/Bo2/Bo3 and two per team in Bo5; settings must declare how official/platform faults count. Requests must meet the published game-clock cutoff.
4. An ordinary rule amendment applies prospectively to future/unplayed games only. Never recalculate completed games under a new rule. Emergency safety/security/platform action requires reason, scope, authorizer, timestamps, affected games, notice to both team representatives, and independent review where feasible.
5. Record the evidence, other side’s response, decision-maker and conflict check, ruling, appeal route, outcome and closeout.

## 6. Evidence, scores, replays and awards

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Reconcile official results and outstanding disputes before finalizing awards or closing records.</div>
1. Each participant supplies a readable original post-battle evidence screen required by the settings. A team representative may coordinate intake but does not replace participant evidence unless an approved exception says so.
2. Enable and preserve replays for at least 48 hours as the recurring source baseline, subject to event settings and applicable platform/storage limitations. State the official retention/deletion period in the settings record.
3. Treat OCR text as a candidate. A human verifies every critical score against the original screenshot; ambiguities remain unresolved until corrected. Preserve original evidence, accepted values, reviewer, correction history and timestamps.
4. Keep accepted participant metrics, individual `dblns` output, scoring calculation, verified match outcome, bracket decision, and award as distinct but linked records. The approved calculation of individual performance metrics determines the match outcome, and that verified outcome governs bracket advancement under D21.
5. Publish award eligibility, exact prizes/values and distribution before play. Do not treat Doubloons or the illustrative dollar display as cash value.

## 7. Communications, conduct and escalation

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Confirm eligibility, identity, and roster against the selected format before match setup.</div>
1. Publish functional communication routes: official announcements/rules; schedule/fixtures; registration/waitlist; help/escalation; evidence/results; roles/access; team communication; optional observer/streaming functions. Exact channel names belong in the event settings.
2. Each team names a primary and backup representative. Participants keep the official route accessible and notify both the opponent and an official promptly of match-impacting technical issues. Provide a text/manual fallback for inaccessible or failed automation.
3. Use impartial decision-makers where possible. A staff member with a conflict must disclose it and hand the decision to a non-conflicted official when feasible. Apply stated conduct/sanction rules and appeal routes consistently.

<div style="background-color:#DFF2FF; border:1.5px solid #0070C0; padding:6px 8px; margin:4px 0;"><strong>⚓ Quick hint:</strong> Reconcile official results and outstanding disputes before finalizing awards or closing records.</div>

3. Archive the issued rules/settings, fixtures, evidence index, approved workbook/build identity, test record, incidents, rulings, appeals and approvals under the declared retention policy.
